How a run works

Four steps.
That’s it.

01

Show up early

Get to the meeting point about five minutes before. Look for the UTRC shirts and the very obvious crowd of eighty people. Say hi — someone will find you.

02

Run your pace

Run at whatever pace feels comfortable, not the one that sounds impressive. There is a sweeper at the back, so going steady never means going alone.

03

Run the loop

There is a leader at the front and a sweeper at the back. The sweeper never passes you. That’s the whole point of the club.

04

Stay for the photo

We regroup at the finish, take the group photo, and usually end up somewhere with coffee or food. The run is the excuse; this bit is the club.

Kit list

What to
bring

Essential

  • Running shoes you’ve worn before
  • Water — bring more than you think in September
  • Layers you can tie round your waist
  • Your phone, for the group photo

Nice to have

  • Open-ear headphones, so you can still hear traffic and the group
  • A light or reflective layer for the 7 PM runs once it gets dark
  • Transit fare, in case the café is further than you planned
  • A small bag — we can usually keep it together at the meeting point

Dressing for it

What to wear,
by temperature

Dress for about ten degrees warmer than the thermometer says. You will be cold for the first kilometre and glad of it for the other four.

10°C and up

Mild

Most of September, and again from May.

  • Shorts, T-shirt or singlet
  • Cap and sunglasses for the Saturday morning run
  • Sunscreen — Front Campus has no shade
  • More water than you think you need
0°C to 10°C

Cool

Late autumn and early spring. The easiest running weather there is.

  • Long-sleeve top, shorts or light tights
  • Thin gloves — hands feel it first
  • A light windbreaker you can tie round your waist
  • Reflective layer for the 7 PM runs once it gets dark early
−15°C to 0°C

Cold

A normal Toronto winter. Layers matter more than thickness.

  • Thermal base layer, then a mid layer over it
  • Full-length tights
  • Proper gloves, a hat over your ears, a neck gaiter
  • Wool or synthetic socks — never cotton once it is wet
Below −15°C

Deep cold

Windchill does the damage, not the number. Cover everything.

  • Two layers up top plus a windproof shell
  • Lined tights, or tights under trackpants
  • Gaiter over your face, mittens over gloves
  • Check Instagram before you leave — extreme cold moves or cancels a run

No. UTRC is a drop-in club — turn up at the meeting point on any run day and you’re in. There’s no form, no waiting list and no commitment to come back.

As fast as you are. Everyone sets off together and runs their own pace, and a sweeper stays at the back and never passes anybody. If you need to walk, the sweeper walks with you. That is what “no one left behind” actually means in practice.

Yes — that’s the group we’re proudest of. Join the social group, take walk breaks whenever you want, and treat the distance as a target rather than a requirement. A lot of our regulars ran their first ever 5K on a Wednesday with us.

Drop-in runs are free. Some special events — race entries, socials, kit — have a cost, and we always say so up front on Instagram.

We’re a University of Toronto student club, and our runs are built around the UofT community — students across every faculty, plus staff and alumni. If you’re outside that and want to run with us, message us on Instagram first and we’ll sort it out.

We run. The only things that stop us are genuinely unsafe conditions — ice storms, lightning, extreme cold warnings — and if a run is cancelled we post it on Instagram stories a few hours beforehand. Check there before you leave.

We’d rather you didn’t wear anything that seals your ears — you need to hear traffic, your leader and the person next to you. Open-ear headphones are a good compromise, and most people find they don’t want music once they’re running in a pack of eighty.

Almost everybody’s first run is on their own. Find any exec or run leader at the start, tell them it’s your first time, and they’ll put you with people running your pace. You will not be standing on your own for long.

Usually. Saturdays finish at a partner café with a discount for runners, and weekday runs often spill into food somewhere nearby. Stick around — it’s where the club actually happens.

Come to runs consistently, get to know the group, then talk to our VP Internal — applications open at the start of each term. We currently have twelve leaders and we’re always short on sweepers for the social group.
